## Fact check (sources)

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| RSUs usually vest over ~4 years, often with a 1-year cliff; taxed as ordinary income at vest | Common plan design; IRS (supplemental wages) |
| Federal supplemental withholding at a flat 22% (37% above $1M) | IRS Publication 15 |
| ISOs must be exercised within 3 months of leaving to keep ISO status; many plans expire options 90 days after you leave | Internal Revenue Code §422; common plan terms |
| The Peter principle, Laurence J. Peter, 1969 | Wikipedia (Peter principle) |
| The shift from pensions (defined benefit) to 401(k)s (defined contribution) | Wikipedia (Pension; 401(k)) |
| FAS 123(R) required companies to expense stock options (effective for most companies from 2006) | FASB; Wikipedia (Employee stock option) |
| Microsoft ended stock options for employees and moved to stock awards in 2003 | Widely reported (July 2003) |
| Enron: shares around $90 in 2000, under $1 by late 2001; employees lost retirement savings held in Enron stock | Wikipedia (Enron scandal) |
| Tech layoffs: Meta ~21,000 (Nov 2022 + Mar 2023), Google ~12,000 (Jan 2023), Amazon ~27,000 across rounds | Wikipedia (2022–2023 layoffs in the tech industry) |
| WHO: burnout is an "occupational phenomenon" (ICD-11, 2019) | World Health Organization |
| 10% early-withdrawal penalty before 59½; rule of 55 | IRS (Topic 558) |
| FTC non-compete rule (April 2024) set aside by a federal court in Texas (Aug 2024); never took effect | Wikipedia (Ryan LLC v. FTC) |
| California bans most employee non-competes | Cal. Business & Professions Code §16600 |
| Make-whole (buyout) grants to replace forfeited stock | Common hiring practice |
