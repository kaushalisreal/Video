## The money math (keep consistent everywhere)

| Item | Number | How it's calculated |
|---|---|---|
| Company value at IPO | $40B | — |
| Your stake | 25% = **$10B** | — |
| Yearly spending | **$100M** | borrowed every year |
| Tax if you sold $100M instead | **$23.8M** federal; **~$37M** in California | 20% long-term capital gains + 3.8% net investment income tax; CA top rate 13.3% |
| Loan interest | about **5%** = **$5M/year** on $100M | example rate for very wealthy borrowers |
| Share growth | **7%/year** = $700M in year one | illustrative average, not a promise |
| Doubling time at 7% | about **10 years** | 1.07^10 ≈ 1.97 |
| Debt after 30 years | **≈ $6.6B** | $100M × ((1.05^30 − 1) / 0.05) = $100M × 66.4 |
| Shares after 30 years | **≈ $76B** | $10B × 1.07^30 = $10B × 7.61 |
| Debt as a share of the fortune | under 10% | 6.6 / 76 ≈ 8.7% |
| The crash | −45% in six weeks, 12 years in | fiction; only *pledged* shares trigger the margin call |
| Rosa the nurse | $85,000 salary; "almost one dollar in five" to federal income tax + Social Security + Medicare | ≈ $9.6K income tax + ≈ $6.5K FICA ≈ 19% |

## Fact check (sources)

| Claim in the script | Source |
|---|---|
| Unrealized gains aren't taxed; tax is due when you sell | Standard US "realization" rule |
| A loan isn't taxable income | Standard US tax treatment of debt |
| Step-up in basis at death | IRC §1014 |
| Estate tax: **$15,000,000** exemption in 2026, top rate 40% | IRS, "What's new — Estate and gift tax" (the Working Families Tax Cuts law, Public Law 119-21, signed July 4, 2025) |
| Top federal long-term capital gains rate 20% + 3.8% NIIT = 23.8% | IRS |
| California top income tax rate 13.3% (capital gains taxed as ordinary income) | California FTB |
| Some founders take a $1 salary (e.g., Mark Zuckerberg) | Meta proxy statements |
| 25 richest Americans: wealth +$401B (2014–2018), $13.6B federal income tax, **3.4% "true tax rate"** | ProPublica, "The Secret IRS Files" (June 2021) |
| A major energy CEO was forced to sell almost all his shares within days in 2008 to cover loans | Chesapeake Energy's Aubrey McClendon, October 2008 (widely reported). Not named in the script. |
| Proposals to tax unrealized gains of the richest haven't become law | e.g., the "Billionaire Minimum Income Tax" proposal (2022), not enacted |

⚠️ The founder, the company, Victoria, Daniel, Rosa and Lily are fictional. Only the rules and the ProPublica figures are real.
