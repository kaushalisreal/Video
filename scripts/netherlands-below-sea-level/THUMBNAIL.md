## 2. Patterns

1. **Show the impossible in one picture: water higher than the houses.** All three big Netherlands breakouts from small channels (232x, 44x, 20x) use the same layout. On one side is a wall/dike with the sea at the top edge, often with a wave. On the other side, a town with red roofs sits clearly *lower*. A viewer understands "this country is below the sea" in under a second, without reading anything.
2. **Above-water vs below-water cutaways win** (Venice 256x, "Too Late?" 30x). Splitting the frame at the waterline into "surface" and "what's under" creates a curiosity gap.
3. **A single number with a scale marker:** "7m" with a measuring arrow, "2 Meters Below Sea Level", "$12 BILLION". The number makes the danger feel real.
4. **Very little text: 2–5 words, often a question that sounds sceptical** ("HOW IS THIS EVEN SAFE?", "WHAT'S UNDER VENICE?", "TOO LATE?"). Thumbnails that repeat the full title as text (Real Engineering, History Scope, Johnny Harris) did average or worse.
5. **Look: bright, saturated, very realistic 3D.** Turquoise/deep-blue sea, orange-red Dutch roofs, green fields, clear daylight, high camera angle. Flat cartoons and plain maps underperform unless the channel is already big.
6. **Threat implied, disaster not shown.** The wave is *about to* hit, and the town is still dry. Tension works better than a picture of the disaster happening.
7. **No faces needed.** None of the outliers here use a presenter's face, which suits a faceless narration channel.

## 3c. Edit prompts: show the water is higher than the ground (approved image: boy + cruise ship)

Problem: in the approved image the ship sits on the grass, so it reads as "a ship on a hill", not "the sea is above the street". These edits make the water visible. Start every edit with the lock line, change one thing per edit, and keep the result that looks most real.

**Lock line, put it first in every edit:**
`Edit this image. Keep everything exactly the same: the boy and bicycle, the brick houses, the street, the cruise ship, the sky, the lighting, the colours and the camera angle. Only change the green dike area as described:`

1. **Cutaway dike (top pick).** Cut a clean vertical cross-section into the left part of the green dike, like a slice taken out of a cake, revealing layers of grass, clay and sand, and behind them deep blue-green sea water. The water surface sits at the top of the dike, exactly at the cruise ship's waterline, far above the boy's head and the street. Realistic water with small waves and light shining through it.
2. **Glass dike / aquarium.** Turn the green grass dike into a thick transparent glass wall, like a giant aquarium. Behind the glass, the sea fills it almost to the top, with turquoise water, light rays, seaweed and a few fish swimming. The water surface is level with the cruise ship's waterline, clearly higher than the street, the boy and the house doors.
3. **Water at the top edge.** Make the top of the dike lower and flatter so a thin strip of blue sea water is visible right at the crest, with small waves lapping at the edge and the cruise ship floating in that water. A few splashes of spray come over the top onto the grass. The street and houses stay dry and far below the waterline.
4. **Phantom water line.** Add a semi-transparent light-blue glowing horizontal band across the whole image at the height of the ship's waterline, passing over the houses at roof height, showing where the sea level is. Below the band, add a very faint ghost-blue tint over the street, as if showing where the water would be. Keep it subtle and realistic, like a documentary graphic.
5. **Height arrow.** Add a bright yellow vertical double-headed measuring arrow beside the boy, running from the street up to the ship's waterline at the top of the dike, with a thin dashed yellow horizontal line extending from the waterline across the houses. No numbers, no text.
6. **Wall of water.** Replace the left half of the grass dike with a perfectly vertical wall of clear turquoise sea water, like the parted Red Sea, with sunlight glowing through it. Its top is a calm sea surface where the cruise ship floats, level with the dike. The right half stays as the grass dike. The street at the bottom stays completely dry.
7. **Waves breaking over the crest (tension).** Add dramatic sea waves crashing against the far side of the dike, with white spray rising above the crest and a little water trickling down the grass slope toward the street. The ship floats in rough water at the top of the dike. Keep the sky and sunlight the same.
8. **Steps up to the sea.** Add a narrow stone staircase running up the grass dike from the street to the crest, with a railing. At the top of the stairs, blue sea water is visible right at the top step, with the cruise ship floating just beyond. It shows people walk UP to reach the sea.

Best combo: 1 or 2 for the main effect, then 5 as a second edit so a number (e.g. "6.7 m") can be added next to the arrow.

## 3d. Edit prompt: see-through dike (user's chosen approach)

Main prompt:
```
Edit this image. Keep everything exactly the same: the boy and bicycle, the brick houses, the street, the cruise ship, the sky, the lighting, the colours and the camera angle.

Only change the green grass dike on the left. Make the TOP HALF of the dike semi-transparent, about 40% opacity, like looking through frosted glass, so the sea behind it is visible through the grass. The bottom half of the dike stays solid green grass, with a soft gradient fade between the solid and the see-through part.

Through the transparent part, show deep blue-green sea water filling the dike right up to its top edge. The water surface is a flat horizontal line exactly at the crest of the dike, at the same height as the cruise ship's waterline. The underwater part of the ship's white hull is visible through the water below that line, so it is clear the ship is floating. Sunlight makes soft rays and ripples in the water.

The water is clearly higher than the street, the boy and the front doors of the houses, but it stays contained behind the dike: no water on the street, everything in front of the dike stays dry.
```

Variants:
- Stronger effect: change "about 40% opacity" to "about 20% opacity" and add "a thin white glowing line marks the water surface along the dike crest".
- Subtler and more realistic: change it to "about 60% opacity" and "the water is only faintly visible through the grass, like a ghost image".
- If the boy or houses change, repeat "Do not change anything outside the green dike area." at the end of the prompt.

Manual fallback (exact control, free in Photopea): generate a version with the dike replaced by water (same prompt, but "replace the top half of the dike with sea water"). Put the water version underneath the original as two layers, add a layer mask to the original, and paint a soft black-to-white gradient over the top half of the dike at 40-60% opacity.

## 4. Text tips (when you add it)
- 2–5 words, all caps, heavy sans-serif (Montserrat Black / Anton), white with a dark stroke or a yellow highlight on one word.
- Don't repeat the video title. Write the sceptical question the picture raises.
- Add one number with a measuring arrow if possible (7 m, 22 ft, −6.7 m).
- Test at 160x90 px. If the sea-above-houses idea isn't obvious at that size, push the height difference further.

## 5. Title

Pick: **This Country Should Be at the Bottom of the Sea** (straight from the script's hook at 0:09)
Thumbnail text pairing: "THE SEA IS ABOVE THEM". Title and thumbnail should add to each other, never repeat.

A/B alternatives:
- Why the Netherlands Should Be Underwater (But Isn't): includes the search keyword
- How the Dutch Built a Country Below the Sea: mirrors the 20x outlier title
- The Dutch Drowned Their Own Country to Win a War: history angle; use with the soldier thumbnail
